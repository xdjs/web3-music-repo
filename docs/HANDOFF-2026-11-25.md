## Playback performance: inspected code and proposed next work

Pete asked why some songs take a long time to start. He rejected a separate compressed listening copy; preserve original audio. Current code was inspected in `src/archive.js`, `src/media-preload.mjs`, `src/next-track-buffer.mjs`, `src/media-recovery.js`:

- Idle/intent preload warms metadata for one audio candidate on the real player after 650 ms. It cancels incomplete warming after 2 seconds, limits starts to 2/minute, and skips data-saver/slow network conditions. One visible candidate can prevent a later hover candidate from replacing it. This does not deliberately preload several seconds of audio.
- One next-track standby targets 30 seconds audio or 8 seconds video, once current playback has 15 seconds headroom. The same media element is adopted to preserve its native buffer. Native preload is a hint, not a hard download cap; browsers can restrict it.
- Initial slow-source fallback is 12 seconds; subsequent checks 18 seconds. Useful buffered progress is preserved to avoid throwing away downloaded media.
- Known live/indexed sources play directly. Other recovery states first call `/api/recover/:id`.

Proposed, **not implemented**: measure click-to-first-audio/stalls and gateway latency; prioritize the actual intent track; improve safe same-content gateway selection; assess caching byte-identical originals with Range/seeking support. Measure before/after in the browser, avoiding duplicate audio, excessive downloads, broken seeking, lost native buffers or starving the current song. Do not just lower every timeout: a slow but progressing original can be damaged by aggressive switches.
