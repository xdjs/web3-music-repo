# NFT collection research — 3 November 2026

38 selected Ethereum contracts, each with a featured edition. This is not a complete census of any artist or platform. A contract can contain many songs or many editions of one song. The collection browser pages through its public index; playback separately reads the selected token on Ethereum.

## Evidence boundaries

Public holdings supplied discovery leads. Artist association follows token metadata and linked release records. No artist wallet-control claim is established by this catalogue. Holdings, deployers, recipients, royalty beneficiaries and collaborators must remain separate roles. Shared contracts do not automatically establish collaboration. Raw wallet inventory and full metadata descriptions are excluded from the public client.

CHAOS is a stronger contract link: the verified Packs contract [0xb7b05231cccfd862b57d7d7a0a0db48e078b504c](https://etherscan.io/address/0xb7b05231cccfd862b57d7d7a0a0db48e078b504c) returns Songs 0x8427e46826a520b1264b55f31fcb5ddfdc31e349 through songContract(). Songs token 4704 names Walls and credits Lackhoney, Boy Untitled and Three Oscillators. [Original contract code](https://github.com/songcamp/campchaos).

## Remaining research

1. Establish self-published artist wallet links, ENS/profile links and original mint records before assigning wallet control. Record source, chain, role and date for every association.
2. Enumerate artist-created collections from those verified identities, separately from collected NFTs. Cross-check shared factories and platform contracts; a platform deployer is not necessarily the artist.
3. Extend beyond Ethereum to the chains used by Sound, Zora and Mint Songs. ERC-1155 URI handling is not implemented. Historical mint-event enumeration and all-platform contract discovery remain unfinished.
4. Recover the complete CHAOS roster and reconcile artist credits against original releases and participant records. Do not identify participants solely through a token holding.
5. Resolve missing artists above; add source-backed contracts deliberately. Test original media and preserve unavailable works as historical records without replacement audio.

The runtime does not crawl connected wallets or claim full platform coverage. It only reads explicitly selected contracts.

## Additional primary-source discovery — 3 November

[Erin Ivey’s own Mint Songs guide](https://erinivey.com/pages/how-to-collect-your-first-music-nft-step-by-step) was read in full. She dates her first mint to 31 December 2021, describes 50 editions, links original Mint Songs song 2321, and identifies Polygon as the chain. Her account describes personal onboarding help and a delayed listing caused by syncing. The page is an attributed artist account, not independently verified transaction evidence. The original linked song ID is a platform identifier, not an established token ID. Contract/token and wallet-control verification remain open; no playable token has been invented from this lead.
